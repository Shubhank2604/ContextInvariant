# ContextOS v0.5.0 research-readiness report

## Decision

Phase 5 supports the narrow research hypothesis that explicit preservation contracts,
relational consistency, and validated transformations can reduce constraint failures under
token pressure. It does **not** yet support a paper-level claim that ContextOS generally
improves long-context task quality.

The implementation is suitable for a `v0.5.0` research-preview release. A paper submission
should wait for held-out and realistic external validation. Feature development is frozen at
Git SHA `f1e92ff39212ee4996273949e21d2f9caa375c20`; the frozen v0.4 reference is
`4fdd88391300c56ad17af5458897ccdd08d6f7bf`.

## Primary controlled result

At the 80% budget, Full Phase 5 and the frozen v0.4 strategy received the same effective
budget on the same 90 generated constraint cases.

| Metric | Frozen v0.4 | Full Phase 5 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Selection constraint violations | 77.78% | 11.11% | -66.67 points; 85.7% relative reduction |
| GPT-5.4-mini answer violations | 22.22% | 11.11% | -11.11 points; 50.0% relative reduction |
| GPT-5.4 answer violations | 33.33% | 11.11% | -22.22 points; 66.7% relative reduction |
| Mean context reduction vs full context | 27.20% | 32.38% | +5.18 points |
| Successful executions | 90/90 | 80/90 | -10 explicit infeasibilities |
| Task score on matched successful cases, GPT-5.4-mini | 75.42% | 100% | +24.58 points |
| Task score on matched successful cases, GPT-5.4 | 68.75% | 100% | +31.25 points |

For GPT-5.4-mini, the paired task-score delta is `+0.2458` with 95% CI
`[0.1542, 0.3375]`. The answer-violation delta is `-0.1111` with CI
`[-0.2222, 0.0111]`, which includes zero.

For GPT-5.4, the paired task-score delta is `+0.3125` with 95% CI
`[0.2248, 0.4125]`. The answer-violation delta is `-0.2222` with CI
`[-0.3444, -0.0889]`, which excludes zero.

The ten unsuccessful Full Phase 5 executions are all multi-hop cases whose protected closure
does not fit the 80% budget. They are explicit overflows, count as violations in the aggregate,
and are not assigned invented task scores. All 80 feasible executions preserved the required
answer values on both model snapshots. This is a safety/coverage trade-off: Phase 5 refuses an
infeasible context instead of silently violating its contracts.

At the 100% budget, Full Phase 5 completed 90/90 cases, retained 100% controlled task score,
reduced selection violations from 55.56% to zero, and reduced context by 8.12%. On
GPT-5.4-mini, frozen v0.4 produced 2.22% answer violations while Full Phase 5 produced none.

## Budget frontier and attribution

The final deterministic sweep covers 25%, 35%, 50%, 65%, 80%, and 100% budgets. It shows a
clear feasibility frontier: stricter contracts cause explicit infeasibility at low budgets,
then reach 50/90 successful cases at 65%, 80/90 at 80%, and 90/90 at 100%. The system does not
silently relax mandatory or relational requirements to manufacture coverage.

Full Phase 5 has local p95 optimizer latency of 5.74 ms at the 80% budget and 4.08 ms at the
100% budget in the final deterministic run. Model-backed constraint runs measured p95 values
of 4.28 ms on GPT-5.4-mini and 4.43 ms on GPT-5.4 at the 80% budget. These measurements exclude
provider latency.

The cumulative Phase 5 variants are behaviorally identical at the 65%, 80%, and 100% frontier
points. The study therefore establishes a system-level difference from frozen v0.4 but does
not isolate a unique contribution from each Phase 5 component. Stronger targeted ablations are
required before making component-level causal claims.

## External LongBench result

The standard LongBench subset contains 25 cases from each of 2WikiMQA, HotpotQA,
PassageRetrieval-en, and RepoBench-P. The final artifact has 298 successful predictions and two
explicit Full Context RepoBench-P context overflows. Twenty-eight transient provider failures
were retried with provenance preserved.

| Dataset | Full Context | Frozen v0.4 | Full Phase 5 | Phase 5 vs Full, 95% CI | Phase 5 context reduction vs Full |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2WikiMQA F1 | 0.5844 | 0.6284 | 0.6604 | +0.0760 `[-0.0360, 0.2000]` | 13.08% |
| HotpotQA F1 | 0.7470 | 0.6430 | 0.5716 | -0.1753 `[-0.3400, -0.0386]` | 37.87% |
| Passage Retrieval | 1.0000 | 0.8400 | 0.8800 | -0.1200 `[-0.2410, 0.0000]` | 37.28% |
| RepoBench-P similarity | 0.0578 | 0.0460 | 0.0592 | +0.0026 `[-0.0509, 0.0574]` | 29.41% on 23 matched cases |

Across the 98 cases for which both Full Context and Full Phase 5 succeeded, Phase 5 used
687,678 context tokens versus 1,005,038, a 31.58% reduction. This token aggregate must not be
presented as an aggregate quality score because the four datasets use different metrics.

Full Phase 5 is numerically above frozen v0.4 on 2WikiMQA, Passage Retrieval, and RepoBench-P,
and below it on HotpotQA; none of those Phase-5-versus-v0.4 confidence intervals excludes zero.
Against Full Context, the HotpotQA degradation is statistically non-zero under the recorded
paired bootstrap interval. The result is mixed external evidence, not universal superiority.

LongBench p95 optimizer latency ranges from 187.95 to 325.05 ms for Full Phase 5, substantially
above the sub-10-ms controlled-fixture measurements. The difference reflects much longer
contexts and embedding/deduplication work, so a universal sub-10-ms latency claim is invalid.

## Positional result

The real-provider positional run executed all 60 predictions: three layouts, five evidence
positions, and 4K, 8K, 16K, and 32K token targets. Every cell achieved 100% exact match and a
zero max-minus-min positional gap.

This is a ceiling-effect null result. It verifies that position-aware layout did not regress
exact retrieval, but it does not reproduce the positional degradation reported in *Lost in the
Middle* and cannot show mitigation. There is one observation per experimental cell, so no
confidence interval is reported. Claims of a 40% positional-gap reduction, a reproduction of
the paper, or a measured positional advantage are unsupported.

## Phase 5K gate assessment

| Gate dimension | Observed result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Context reduction | 32.38% controlled at 80%; 31.58% across matched LongBench cases | Useful, below the illustrative 40-50% territory |
| Critical-information retention | 100% on feasible controlled executions; explicit overflow on 10/90 at 80% | Strong controlled result with coverage caveat |
| Structural/context failures | Selection violations reduced 85.7%; answer violations reduced 50.0-66.7% across two model snapshots | Strongest supported result |
| Full-context quality retention | 100% on feasible controlled cases; mixed LongBench outcomes | Not consistent across workloads |
| Optimizer overhead | 4.08-5.74 ms p95 on controlled cases; 187.95-325.05 ms on LongBench | Workload-dependent; no universal low-latency claim |
| Positional robustness | All strategies at ceiling | Null; benchmark did not discriminate layouts |
| Component attribution | Cumulative variants tie at the main feasible frontier | Insufficient for causal component claims |

The Phase 5 mechanism is promising and the primary controlled effect replicates qualitatively
across two OpenAI model snapshots. The overall paper-readiness gate is **not passed** because
the constraint set co-evolved with the implementation, external task results are mixed, the
positional test is non-discriminating, and component attribution is weak.

## Supported claims

- ContextOS implements a model-agnostic, constraint-aware context construction runtime with
  preservation contracts, hard relations, omission-risk-aware allocation, validated
  transformations, explicit infeasibility, and auditable traces.
- On the 90-case controlled development benchmark at an 80% budget, Full Phase 5 reduced
  selection violations from 77.78% to 11.11%, an 85.7% relative reduction versus frozen v0.4.
- On the same benchmark, answer violations fell by 50.0% on GPT-5.4-mini and 66.7% on GPT-5.4.
- Full Phase 5 preserved all required values on every feasible controlled execution while
  reducing context by 32.38% at the 80% budget.
- The project includes reproducible budget sweeps, cumulative ablations, paired bootstrap
  intervals, external LongBench evaluation, raw predictions, and provider/runtime telemetry.

## Unsupported claims

- ContextOS universally matches or beats Full Context, relevance-only selection, or frozen
  v0.4 on general long-context tasks.
- ContextOS preserves a single aggregate percentage of “LongBench quality.”
- ContextOS reproduces or mitigates *Lost in the Middle*.
- Every individual Phase 5 mechanism has an independently measured causal contribution.
- The optimizer universally runs below 10 ms p95.
- The current results establish state of the art or paper-ready external validity.

## Work required before a paper submission

1. Freeze and evaluate a newly authored held-out constraint set that was not used during
   algorithm development.
2. Add at least one realistic long-running agent workload with evolving tool state,
   supersession, dependencies, and exact-value requirements.
3. Validate with another provider/model family, not only two snapshots from one family.
4. Compare against stronger model-backed context-selection or compression baselines under
   equal budgets.
5. Randomize or counterbalance strategy execution to reduce prompt-cache and temporal provider
   confounding.
6. Add targeted ablations whose cases actually distinguish preservation contracts, hard
   relations, validation, and omission-risk allocation.
7. Redesign the positional task or add repetitions only if positional mitigation remains a
   paper claim; otherwise report the null and remove that claim from the thesis.
8. Perform structured error analysis on the ten infeasible multi-hop cases and the HotpotQA
   degradation without tuning against the held-out set.
9. Retain a public evidence package and have the report independently reproduced.

## Release recommendation

Proceed with `v0.5.0` as a research preview after documentation, public evidence retention,
packaging/install checks, CI, and release metadata are complete. Do not label this `v1.0.0`,
do not resume algorithm tuning before the held-out protocol is fixed, and do not present this
report as a completed paper.
